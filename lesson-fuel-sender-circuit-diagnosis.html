How the Fuel Sender Works

The fuel level sender uses a float connected to a variable resistor. As the float rises and falls with fuel level, the resistance changes, and the instrument cluster or PCM converts that resistance into a fuel level reading.

Slide 2 of 3
Common Fuel Sender Problems
  • A worn resistor track causes an inaccurate or jumpy reading, since the wiper loses consistent contact as it passes over worn spots.
  • A stuck float reads a constant, unchanging level no matter how much fuel is actually in the tank.
  • Wiring or connector corrosion between the sender and the cluster can cause an erratic or completely dead reading.
Slide 3 of 3
Isolating the Problem
Check sender resistance directly at the sender's connector, at a known full and known empty position if possible, and compare to spec. If the sender itself reads correctly but the gauge still doesn't, the problem is in the wiring or the cluster, not the sender.
