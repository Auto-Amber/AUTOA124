Valve Spring Interpretation

What Free Length Means

Free length is a valve spring's length while it is fully relaxed and uncompressed, outside the cylinder head. It is measured with a caliper or on a dedicated spring tester and compared against the specified free length in service information.

This is different from installed height or open/closed pressure, which are measured with the spring compressed to a specific length instead.

Slide 2 of 3
Why Free Length Drops

Valve springs lose tension over time from repeated heat cycles and the simple mileage of opening and closing thousands of times per minute of engine run time. That loss of tension shows up as a measured free length that comes in below the new-spec value.

A spring can look physically fine, no cracks, no obvious damage, and still have lost enough tension to fail a free length check.
Slide 3 of 3
What a Low Reading Means and What to Do

A spring measuring below spec free length has also lost seat pressure and open pressure, even though those aren't what you directly measured. That loss can allow valve float at higher RPM and reduces how tightly the spring keeps the valve seated.

Compare the measurement against the wear limit in service information. If it's below the limit, the standard practice is to replace the springs as a full set, not just the one that failed, so pressures stay matched across every cylinder.
