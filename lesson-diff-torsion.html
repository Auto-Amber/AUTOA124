Gear-Based Torque Bias

A torsion differential, such as a Torsen, uses worm and helical gears instead of clutch plates. The gear friction lets it send more torque to the wheel with more traction.

The ratio of how much more torque goes to the high-traction side is called the torque bias ratio.

Slide 2 of 3
Strengths and Limits
  • Smooth, quiet, and no clutches to wear.
  • Reacts without delay and needs no friction modifier in most cases.
  • Needs some resistance at both wheels. If one wheel is in the air or on a very slick surface, little torque can transfer.
Slide 3 of 3
Service

Use the fluid the manufacturer specifies. Look for metal in the fluid and check for noise or binding in tight turns.

A wheel lifted off the ground does not mean a torsion differential is bad. It has nothing to push against.
