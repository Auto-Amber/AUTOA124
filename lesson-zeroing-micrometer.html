Why Zero Matters

A micrometer only reads correctly if its zero point is true. Drops, normal wear on the anvil and spindle faces, and temperature swings can all shift that zero without any obvious sign on the tool.

That is why checking zero happens before you trust a reading, not after you already wrote a number on the lab sheet. If the zero is off, every reading that mic gives you is off by the same amount.

Slide 2 of 3
Checking and Adjusting a 0-1 Inch Micrometer

Clean the anvil and spindle faces first. A speck of dirt between them will give you a false reading before you even get to the zero check.

  • Close the mic gently using the ratchet or friction thimble, never by cranking the spindle down by hand.
  • Look at where the line on the thimble lines up with the index line on the sleeve.
  • If it lines up exactly at zero, the mic is true.
  • If it does not, use the spanner wrench that fits the adjustment hole on the sleeve to rotate the sleeve until the lines match, then close and check again.
Using the ratchet instead of hand pressure on the spindle gives you the same closing force every time. Hand pressure varies and can wear the faces unevenly.
Slide 3 of 3
Zeroing Mics Larger Than 1 Inch

A 2 to 3 inch micrometer (or any range above 0 to 1 inch) cannot close all the way to zero on itself. For those, you zero against a certified gauge standard sized for that mic’s range.

Close the mic on the standard the same careful way, using the ratchet, and adjust the sleeve the same way if it is off. Never force the adjustment. If a mic is consistently off after adjustment, flag it for calibration or replacement rather than trusting a workaround.
