Two Different Wear Patterns

A cylinder bore does not always wear evenly, and two different wear patterns matter for diagnosis:

  • Taper - the diameter changes along the length of the bore. Usually largest near the top of ring travel, where heat and friction are highest, and smaller lower down.
  • Out-of-round - the diameter differs depending on which direction you measure, at the same depth. Commonly shows up between the thrust side and the push side of the piston.
Slide 2 of 3
How to Measure Each One

These two wear patterns need two different measurement approaches, and you cannot catch either one with a single reading.

To check taper: measure in the same direction at multiple depths, top, middle, and bottom of ring travel. Compare the numbers.
To check out-of-round: measure at the same depth in two perpendicular directions. Compare the numbers.
Slide 3 of 3
Why This Matters

Taper and out-of-round each have their own spec limit, separate from the bore's average diameter. A bore can have an average diameter that lands right in the middle of spec and still have excessive taper or out-of-round hiding underneath that average.

If either measurement exceeds its limit, the cylinder needs machining regardless of what the average diameter says on its own.
