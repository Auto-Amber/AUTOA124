How the Hydraulic Clutch Works

A hydraulic clutch uses a master cylinder on the pedal, a line, and a slave cylinder at the transmission. Pressing the pedal creates fluid pressure that moves the slave cylinder, which moves the release fork or release bearing.

The slave may be a separate unit or built into the release bearing around the input shaft.

Slide 2 of 3
Common Symptoms
  • A soft or spongy pedal
  • A pedal that goes to the floor, or sinks slowly
  • Hard shifting or grinding because the clutch will not fully release
  • A low fluid level in the reservoir
These usually point to air in the system or a leak at the master cylinder, slave cylinder, or line.
Slide 3 of 3
Diagnosis and Bleeding
  • Check the reservoir level and condition, and use the fluid the manufacturer specifies.
  • Look for leaks at the master cylinder, the slave cylinder, and along the line.
  • Bleed the system by the service information procedure. Some systems with an internal slave need a specific method.
  • After the repair, confirm the clutch releases fully and the pedal feels normal.
