Cooling System Diagnostic Tests

Slide 1 of 3
Cooling System Pressure Test

Pressurize the system to the cap's rated pressure, hold, and watch the gauge.

  • A steady hold means the system is sealing correctly.
  • A dropping gauge with no visible puddle under the vehicle points toward an internal leak (such as a head gasket) or a leak in a hard-to-see location, and needs follow-up like a coolant dye test.
Slide 2 of 3
Galvanic Corrosion Test

This test measures stray voltage in the coolant with a DVOM, one probe in the coolant, the other grounded to the battery negative terminal.

A reading above the manufacturer's spec, commonly under 0.5V, means the coolant's additive package is breaking down and can no longer protect the system's metal components from electrolytic corrosion.
Slide 3 of 3
Why Both Tests Matter Together

A pressure test checks mechanical seal integrity, whether the system holds pressure. A galvanic test checks the coolant's chemistry, whether it's still actively protecting metal components.

A system can pass one test and fail the other completely independently. Both results belong in the customer recommendation, since they point to different problems with different fixes.
