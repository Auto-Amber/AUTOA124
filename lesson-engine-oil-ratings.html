Engine Oil Ratings

Slide 1 of 3
SAE Viscosity Rating

A rating like 5W-30 describes how the oil flows at two different temperatures.

  • The number before the W is tested at 0°F, cold-start flow behavior. Lower numbers flow easier when cold.
  • The number after the W is tested at 212°F, operating temperature. This is the oil's hot-side viscosity, how well it holds its film strength once the engine is fully warmed up.
W stands for Winter, not weight.
Slide 2 of 3
API Service Classification

The letter code, like SN or SP, describes the oil's chemical formulation and performance standard for gasoline engines. The sequence moves through the alphabet (SA, SB, SC... SN, SP) as formulations improve to protect newer engine technology.

Earlier letters in the sequence become obsolete as engine designs evolve. Always match or exceed the minimum API rating the manufacturer specifies.

Slide 3 of 3
How They Work Together

Both ratings appear together on the bottle, something like "SAE 5W-30, API SN Plus." They describe two separate things:

  • Viscosity rating describes flow behavior at cold and hot temperatures.
  • API rating describes the chemical formulation and what modern engine technology it's built to protect.

Using the wrong viscosity affects flow at temperature extremes. Using an outdated API rating can miss protections newer engines need, like deposit control in turbocharged, direct-injection engines.
