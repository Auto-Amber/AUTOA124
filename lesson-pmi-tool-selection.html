What Counts as a PMI

PMI stands for Precision Measuring Instrument. In Engine Repair you will reach for a small set of these constantly, and picking the wrong one wastes time and can give you a bad reading that leads to a bad diagnosis.

  • Outside micrometer - reads an outside diameter directly (a journal, a shaft, a piston).
  • Telescoping gauge + outside micrometer - reads a bore diameter under about 2 inches (the telescoping gauge transfers the size, the micrometer reads it).
  • Dial bore gauge - reads a bore diameter as a deviation from a set zero, fast at multiple depths and directions.
  • Dial indicator - reads endplay, runout, or warpage as movement against a fixed point.
  • Feeler gauge - reads a gap or clearance between two surfaces.
  • Plastigage - estimates bearing clearance without doing separate bore and journal measurements.

Matching the Tool to the Question

Before you pick up a tool, ask what you are actually trying to find out.

Outside surface? Use an outside micrometer.
One bore reading? Use a telescoping gauge with a micrometer, or an inside micrometer.
Checking a bore for taper or out-of-round at several points fast? Use a dial bore gauge.
Checking shaft endplay or runout? Use a dial indicator.
Checking a gap between two flat or curved surfaces? Use a feeler gauge.
Checking bearing clearance quickly, without a separate bore and journal calculation? Use Plastigage.
Slide 3 of 3
Why This Choice Matters

The wrong tool for the job does not just cost you time. A telescoping gauge walked through a bore one time gives you a single number and hides taper or out-of-round wear that a dial bore gauge would have caught in seconds. A tool that reads slower than it should also invites shortcuts, and shortcuts are where bad measurements get written down as good ones.

Always cross-reference your measurement against the spec in the service information before you decide a part is good or bad. The tool gets you the number. The spec tells you what the number means.
