What Pintle Lift Means

Inside a fuel injector, the pintle is the small valve that lifts off its seat to let fuel spray through. On an injector waveform, a secondary bump or shoulder visible just after the initial voltage spike corresponds to this pintle actually lifting and opening.

Slide 2 of 3
Why This Detail Matters
The main voltage spike shows the circuit is electrically energizing. The pintle lift signature shows the injector is mechanically responding and actually opening. These are two different things, and an injector can show one without the other.
Slide 3 of 3
What a Missing Pintle Signature Suggests

A normal voltage spike with no visible pintle lift bump suggests the injector is receiving the electrical signal correctly, but may not be opening mechanically, a stuck or gummed-up pintle, even though the circuit itself tests fine.

This is a case where electrical testing alone (resistance, basic waveform) can miss a real mechanical fault that only shows up in the detailed waveform shape.
