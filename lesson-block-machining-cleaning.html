Block Machining: Cleaning

Why Clean Comes Before Machining

A block has to be thoroughly cleaned before any machining work, boring, honing, decking, starts. Oil, old gasket material, casting sand, and sludge left in passages or on surfaces cause real problems once cutting begins.

Contamination can dull cutting tools, throw off measurements, and get trapped in oil or coolant passages once the block is reassembled.
Slide 2 of 3
Common Cleaning Methods
  • Hot tank - submerges the block in a heated caustic solution to strip oil, grease, and old gasket residue.
  • Jet washer / parts washer - sprays heated cleaning solution through passages and over surfaces.
  • Media or bead blasting - removes stubborn carbon or corrosion that cleaning solutions alone won't lift.
  • Passage brushing - oil galleries and coolant passages often need a brush pushed through by hand to clear debris a bath alone won't reach.
Slide 3 of 3
Why the Order Matters

Clean, inspect, then machine, in that order. A dirty block hides cracks and surface defects during inspection, and machining a dirty surface can trap debris right under a freshly cut, now-sealed surface where it's much harder to remove later.
