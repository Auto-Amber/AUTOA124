Valve Lash Adjustment Procedure

Progress - 0%
Slide 1 of 3
What Valve Lash Is

Valve lash is the small clearance in the valve train that allows for thermal expansion. On overhead cam engines, it's commonly measured between the camshaft lobe and the follower or rocker.

Too little clearance and valves may not fully seat when hot. Too much and the valve train gets noisy and loses precision in valve timing.

Slide 2 of 3
Where to Measure It
  • Clearance is measured at the follower or rocker, between it and the camshaft lobe, on most OHC engines.
  • The lobe must be on its base circle, the round part opposite the lift ramp, when measuring. Measuring on the ramp gives a false reading.
  • Some engines use shims to adjust clearance, swapped for a different thickness to bring lash into spec.
Slide 3 of 3
Hot or Cold Is Not Universal

A common assumption is that valve lash is always adjusted cold. That's true for many engines, but not all.

Some manufacturers specify hot-engine lash adjustment instead. Always check the specific procedure for the engine you're working on rather than assuming.
