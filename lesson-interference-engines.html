Interference vs Noninterference Engines

Interference vs Noninterference

In an interference engine, the piston at the top of its travel and an open valve can occupy the same space. Correct timing is the only thing keeping them from meeting.

In a noninterference engine, there is enough clearance that a piston cannot reach an open valve, even if timing is lost.

Slide 2 of 3
What a Broken Belt Does
  • Interference engine: the cam stops, some valves are left open, and the crank keeps turning on momentum. Pistons hit the open valves. Expect bent valves and possibly damaged pistons or head.
  • Noninterference engine: the engine stops and will not restart, but there is normally no internal damage. A new belt and correct timing usually fixes it.
Slide 3 of 3
Do Not Assume

Look up whether the engine is interference or noninterference in service information before you decide how risky a broken or slipped belt is.

On an interference engine that has lost timing, do not crank it or turn it over by hand until you have verified timing. Turning it can bend valves that are still good.
