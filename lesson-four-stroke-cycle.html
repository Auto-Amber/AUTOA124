The Four-Stroke Cycle

The Four Strokes, In Order
  • Intake - the piston moves down, the intake valve opens, and an air/fuel mixture is drawn into the cylinder.
  • Compression - both valves close, the piston moves up, and it squeezes that mixture into a smaller space.
  • Power - the spark plug fires, the mixture ignites, and the resulting pressure drives the piston back down.
  • Exhaust - the exhaust valve opens, the piston moves up again, and it pushes the burned gases out of the cylinder.
Slide 2 of 3
Why the Order Cannot Change

Each stroke depends on the one before it being complete.

Intake has to come first, because there is nothing to compress until a mixture is drawn in.
Compression has to come before Power, because the spark ignites a compressed mixture near top dead center. An uncompressed mixture doesn't produce useful power.
Exhaust has to come after Power, because the burned gases have to leave before the next Intake stroke can draw in a fresh mixture.
Slide 3 of 3
Tying It to Crankshaft Rotation

Each stroke corresponds to 180 degrees of crankshaft rotation. Four strokes means one complete cycle takes 720 degrees, or two full crankshaft revolutions.

This is also why the camshaft, which controls valve timing, turns at half crankshaft speed. It only needs to open each valve once per two crank revolutions, not once per revolution.
