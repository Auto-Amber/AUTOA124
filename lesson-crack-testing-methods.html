Crack Testing Methods

Slide 1 of 3
Clean First, Every Time

No crack testing method gives a reliable result on a dirty part. Oil, grease, and carbon deposits can hide a crack or create a false indication.

Clean the part thoroughly before dye penetrant, magnetic particle, or pressure testing, regardless of which method you plan to use.
Slide 2 of 3
Pressure Testing

Pressure testing finds cracks that leak. The part's passages (coolant jackets, oil galleries) are plugged, and the part is pressurized with air or water, often while submerged so leaks show as bubbles.

A crack that leaks under pressure will reveal itself even if it isn't visible on the surface.

Slide 3 of 3
Other Common Methods
  • Dye penetrant - a dye seeps into surface cracks, then a developer draws it back out to make the crack visible.
  • Magnetic particle - works on ferrous parts; a magnetic field is applied and iron particles gather at crack edges.

Each method has its place, but all of them depend on a clean starting surface to give a trustworthy result.
