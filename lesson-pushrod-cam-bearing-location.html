Pushrod Camshaft Bearing Location

Slide 1 of 3
Where the Cam Lives on a Pushrod Engine

On a pushrod (OHV) engine, the camshaft sits down inside the block, not up in the cylinder head. It rides in bearing bores machined directly into the block, usually with separate cam bearing inserts pressed in.

This is different from an overhead cam (OHC) engine, where the camshaft rides in the head itself.

Slide 2 of 3
Why This Changes Block Service
Because the cam bearings live in the block on a pushrod engine, checking and replacing them is part of block service, not head service.

This matters when deciding what work belongs where during a teardown, cam bearing condition gets evaluated right along with the rest of the block's internals.

Slide 3 of 3
Alignment Matters as Much as Clearance

The cam bearing bores have to line up straight through the full length of the block. Even if each individual bearing's clearance checks out fine, a bore that's out of alignment will bind the camshaft as it turns.

Checking bore alignment is a separate step from checking bearing clearance, and both need to pass before the cam goes back in.
