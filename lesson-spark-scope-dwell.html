Spark Scope Pattern and Dwell

Reading a Secondary Pattern

A spark pattern on an ignition scope shows the coil's secondary voltage over time. The main parts are:

  • Firing line: the tall spike showing the voltage needed to jump the plug gap.
  • Spark line (burn time): the flatter section showing how long the spark burns across the gap, often around 1 to 2 milliseconds. Compare to spec.
  • Oscillations: the ringing at the end as leftover coil energy dissipates.
Slide 2 of 3
What Dwell Means
Dwell is the time the coil's primary circuit is switched on, building its magnetic field before the spark. It is measured in milliseconds or crankshaft degrees.

The PCM controls dwell. Too little dwell gives the coil less stored energy and a weaker spark. Too much wastes energy and can overheat the coil.

Slide 3 of 3
What the Pattern Tells You
  • A firing line higher than normal suggests high resistance, such as a wide plug gap or a worn plug.
  • A firing line lower than normal suggests low resistance, such as a fouled or shorted plug.
  • A short burn time or weak oscillations suggest the coil is not delivering enough energy.

Compare to a known good cylinder on the same engine, since normal values vary by system.
