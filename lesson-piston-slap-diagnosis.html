What Piston Slap Is

Piston slap is the audible knock caused by excess piston-to-wall clearance, the piston rocking and striking the cylinder wall. It's distinct from a rod knock (bearing related) or valve train noise, both of which have different causes and different sound patterns.

Slide 2 of 3
The Classic Symptom Pattern
Piston slap is most noticeable at cold start and idle, and it typically quiets down or changes character as the engine warms up.

That pattern happens because clearance is largest when the engine is cold and closes up slightly as the piston expands with heat, often most noticeable on cold mornings.

Slide 3 of 3
Telling It Apart from Other Knocks

Rod knock tends to persist regardless of engine temperature and can change with load. Piston slap is characteristically a cold-engine noise that improves as the engine warms up.

That temperature-based pattern is a useful diagnostic clue technicians use before deciding an engine needs to be torn down.
