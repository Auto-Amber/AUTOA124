Telescoping Gauge Procedure

Progress - 0%
Slide 1 of 3
What a Telescoping Gauge Does

A telescoping gauge is a T-shaped tool with two spring-loaded plungers that expand outward. It transfers a bore diameter to a size you can then read with an outside micrometer. The gauge itself has no scale.

It is typically used for bores under about 2 inches, where a dial bore gauge may not be the right size or a quick single reading is all you need.

Slide 2 of 3
Taking the Measurement
  1. Select a telescoping gauge sized for the bore range you're measuring.
  2. Compress the plungers and insert the gauge into the bore.
  3. Release the plungers so they expand and contact the bore walls.
  4. Rock the gauge slightly to find the true diameter, the widest point straight across, perpendicular to the bore wall.
  5. Lock the plungers in place with the thumbscrew on the handle.
Slide 3 of 3
Transferring to the Micrometer

Once the plungers are locked, carefully remove the gauge from the bore without bumping the plungers out of position.

Immediately measure across the locked plungers with an outside micrometer. The number on the micrometer is your bore diameter.

Do this transfer right away. The longer the gauge sits after you lock it, the more chance there is for something to bump it and change the setting before you get a reading.
