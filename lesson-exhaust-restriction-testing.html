Exhaust Restriction Testing

Slide 1 of 3
What Causes Exhaust Restriction
  • A collapsed or crushed section of pipe
  • A clogged catalytic converter
  • A clogged or packed muffler

Any of these traps exhaust gas and forces the engine to work against backpressure it shouldn't have to fight.

Slide 2 of 3
Backpressure Gauge Method

A backpressure gauge threads into an O2 sensor bung or exhaust port and reads pressure directly, both at idle and at higher RPM.

Always compare the reading to the spec for that exhaust system. Restriction effects are most obvious at higher RPM, since more exhaust volume has to flow through the same restricted opening.
Slide 3 of 3
Vacuum Gauge Method

Watch intake vacuum at idle, then snap the throttle open to a higher RPM and hold it there. On a restricted exhaust, vacuum climbs briefly and then drops as exhaust gas backs up and has nowhere to go.

Symptoms in the field match this pattern too: power loss that gets worse as RPM and load increase, since the restriction's effect grows with exhaust flow volume.
