How to Read a Micrometer

Progress - 0%
Slide 1 of 3
Anatomy of the Scale

A standard outside micrometer has two scales that work together.

  • Sleeve (barrel): the numbered scale running along the top, with a horizontal index line. Each numbered line equals 0.100 inch. Between the numbered lines are smaller unnumbered lines, each equal to 0.025 inch.
  • Thimble: the rotating scale around the barrel, marked with 25 graduations. Each graduation equals 0.001 inch.
Sleeve lines get you to the nearest 0.025 inch. The thimble scale fills in the last three decimal places.
Slide 2 of 3
The Three-Step Read
  1. Read the last fully visible numbered line on the sleeve and multiply by 0.100.
  2. Count any additional small unnumbered lines visible past that number and multiply by 0.025 each.
  3. Read where the thimble’s single line lines up with the sleeve’s horizontal index line, and multiply that graduation by 0.001.

Add all three numbers together for the final reading.

Slide 3 of 3
Worked Example

Say the sleeve shows the number 2 fully visible, plus one small line past it. The thimble lines up at graduation 12.

  • 2 numbered lines × 0.100 = 0.200
  • 1 small line × 0.025 = 0.025
  • Thimble at 12 × 0.001 = 0.012

0.200 + 0.025 + 0.012 = 0.237 inch

A vernier micrometer adds a fourth scale on the sleeve for ten-thousandths, but the sleeve and thimble read the same way described here.
