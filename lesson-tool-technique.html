Two Habits Before Any Reading

Before you trust any precision measurement, two habits come first, every single time, not just at the start of the shift:

  • Zero-check the tool.
  • Make sure the tool's measuring faces and the part are clean.

Skipping either one means you don't actually know if the number you're about to write down is real.

Slide 2 of 3
Why Cleanliness Matters

A chip of metal, a film of oil, or a bit of grit sitting between a tool's measuring faces and the part adds false thickness to the reading. That extra thickness is invisible unless you're specifically looking for it, and it goes straight into your number.

Wipe both the tool's measuring faces and the part's surface before every measurement, not just the first one of the day.
Slide 3 of 3
Why Temperature Matters

Metal expands when it warms up. A part fresh off a running engine, or a tool that's been sitting closed in a warm hand for a while, can read slightly different than the same tool and part at room temperature.

Let hot parts cool to near room temperature before taking a precision measurement whenever the job allows it, and avoid gripping a tool's frame with bare hands longer than necessary during a critical reading.
