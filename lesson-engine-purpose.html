What the Engine Actually Does

An engine is a controlled series of energy conversions, not a single job. It does not store energy (that's the battery's role), and it is not simply "moving parts around."

Its core purpose is to take the chemical energy stored in fuel and convert it, first into heat through combustion, and then into mechanical energy that can do work.

Slide 2 of 3
The Conversion Chain

Follow the energy from the fuel tank to the wheels:

Chemical energy (fuel) → Heat energy (combustion) → Mechanical energy (piston pushed down, crankshaft rotation)

That mechanical energy is what propels the vehicle and drives everything bolted to the front of the engine, the alternator, power steering pump, and AC compressor.

Slide 3 of 3
Why This Framing Helps Diagnosis

When an engine runs poorly, this chain gives you a place to start. Ask which link broke:

  • Is fuel (chemical energy) actually getting into the cylinder?
  • Is it igniting properly and creating heat (combustion)?
  • Is that heat being converted efficiently into force (mechanical integrity, compression)?

A no-start or a rough-running complaint almost always traces back to a break somewhere in this chain.
