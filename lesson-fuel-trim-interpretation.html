Short-Term vs Long-Term Trim
  • Short-term fuel trim (STFT) - a fast, moment-to-moment correction the PCM makes in response to the current oxygen sensor reading.
  • Long-term fuel trim (LTFT) - a slower-moving, learned correction built up over time, based on the pattern of short-term corrections needed.

Positive trim numbers mean the PCM is adding fuel (correcting a lean condition). Negative numbers mean it's subtracting fuel (correcting a rich condition).

Slide 2 of 3
Reading the Pattern
High positive trim at idle that improves at higher RPM often points to a small vacuum leak, since a fixed-size leak has a bigger relative effect on airflow at idle's low total intake volume.

Trim that's off evenly across the whole RPM range points more toward a sensor calibration or fuel delivery issue affecting the whole system equally.

Slide 3 of 3
Why Both Numbers Matter

Short-term trim alone only shows what's happening right now. Long-term trim shows the bigger pattern the PCM has learned. Looking at both together, and how they relate to each other, gives a much clearer diagnostic picture than either number alone.
